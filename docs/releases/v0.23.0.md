## Harbor 0.23.0 — fixes and wishes from moving real apps onto Harbor

Everything in the [0.22.1 notes](https://github.com/carlosalaniz/harbor/blob/main/docs/releases/v0.22.1.md)
still applies. New in this release:

**Encryption, said plainly**

- `harbor install <app>` now installs **encrypted**, exactly like the console's *Local* choice
  (sealed in the Harbor data folder with Harbor's own key). Plain Docker volumes need
  `--unencrypted`, and the plan then says *Not encrypted*.
- `harbor list` has an **ENCRYPTED** column, `harbor inspect` and the app's details say
  `Encrypted: yes/no`. Apps that are not encrypted start on their own after a reboot; the
  operator guide explains how to tell them apart.

**Publishing that no longer breaks updates**

- A **tailnet address gets its own port** instead of reusing the app's port. In LAN mode the old
  way clashed with the app's own listener: the address showed `degraded`, and the app's next update
  could fail and leave it in `needs_action`. Addresses published before 0.23.0 move to their own
  port by themselves after the upgrade; a notification gives the new address.
- **Remove keeps your addresses**: the remove plan lists them, and **Reinstall publishes them
  again** with the same main address.
- **A way out of `needs_action`**: you can withdraw an address while the app is broken, and the new
  **Repair** button (`harbor repair <app>`) runs the app again from its stored release. A failed
  start no longer leaves half-created containers behind.

**Home and packages**

- **Hide from Home** (app details, or `harbor look <app> --hide`) for helpers without a page of
  their own. Home shows a quiet *N hidden apps* link to reach them.
- A fresh install starts with **Bing's picture of the day** as the wallpaper. Existing installs keep
  their choice.
- API-only apps (`kind: api` on the endpoint) show their addresses but no *Open* button.
- For package authors: `harbor packages validate <folder>`, secrets inside an environment value
  (`template: "postgres://app:{{value}}@db/app"`), generated secrets shown once (`showOnce`),
  your own label for a provisioned login, `<` and `>` allowed in text, empty `command:` arguments,
  and `harbor install --credentials-file <path>` to keep one-time logins out of the terminal.
- When port 80 belongs to another program, the log says so (it used to blame Caddy).
